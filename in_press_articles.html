Neighborhood Geometry–Guided Prototype Contrastive Adaptation for Cross-Domain Remote Sensing Scene Classification

Avaliable online: August 17, 2026
Authors: He, Qing; Li, Erzhu; Zhu, Wei; Wang, Yongqing;
Abstract:
Read more... Cross-domain remote sensing scene classification suffers from severe domain shifts caused by sensor and geographic variations. Existing adversarial domain adaptation methods mainly align global feature distributions but often neglect category-level structure, leading to ambiguous decision boundaries in the target domain. In this paper, we propose a neighborhood geometry–guided prototype contrastive adaptation (NGPCA) framework built upon the domain-adversarial neural network. The proposed method leverages local neighborhood geometry in the target feature space to construct neighborhood-consensus pseudo labels and further introduces prototype contrastive learning to align target features with source-domain class prototypes. By combining neighborhood consensus supervision with prototype-level contrastive adaptation, NGPCA improves pseudo-label robustness and promotes more discriminative feature learning. Extensive experiments on University of California, Merced land-use data set, the Aerial Image Data Set, and the NWPU-RESISC45 data set with six cross-domain transfer tasks demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed framework. NGPCA achieves the best average accuracy of 98.26% among the compared remote sensing–oriented and general domain adaptation methods, showing robust performance for cross-domain remote sensing scene classification.

Analytical Estimate of Digital Elevation Model Accuracy (Mean Square Error) as a Function of Cell Size and Dataset Elevation Error

Avaliable online: August 7, 2026
Authors: López-Vázquez, Carlos
Abstract:
Read more... Modern digital elevation models (DEMs) are generated with increasingly small cell sizes and high accuracy, yet no closed-form relationship links these variables. Previous work relied on empirical formulations validated experimentally. This paper derives an analytical relationship for the traditional elevation mean square error (MSE) accuracy metric and highlights limitations of empirical approaches. Common practice wrongly dismisses the effect of the choice of interpolation method on DEM accuracy. Accuracy reports may even fail to mention which interpolation method was used. The analysis was limited to two local interpolation methods: nearest neighbor and bilinear interpolation. In those cases we show that the model accuracy is bounded by three terms: one depending only on the dataset elevation MSE; mixed monomials depending on both the dataset elevation MSE and powers of the cell size h; and a term independent of the dataset elevation MSE and proportional to a power of h. For a given interpolation method, the exponents of h are constant, whereas the coefficients can be estimated from local terrain characteristics at the control points. For bilinear interpolation, the exponents are twice those for nearest-neighbor interpolation. The availability of this analytical relationship makes it possible to determine the cell size or instrument accuracy required to achieve a specified target DEM accuracy. Because the result depends strongly on the interpolation method, the recommended interpolation method should be routinely and explicitly mentioned in the DEM producer’s accuracy report

Mitigating Radiometric Bias in Unmanned Aerial Vehicle Thermal Infrared Imagery Using Temperature-Controlled Reference Plates

Avaliable online: August 7, 2026
Authors: Cervini, Gaia; Jung, Jinha; Roberts, Nicholas A.; Gkritza, Konstantina;
Abstract:
Read more... Temperature retrieval from unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) thermal imagery commonly relies on proprietary manufacturer radiometric calibration software. However, the correction procedures used by these workflows are generally undisclosed, and some user-defined parameters, particularly altitude, are restricted to ranges that do not reflect real-world conditions, risking the introduction of bias and higher radiometric uncertainty. Accordingly, this study evaluates the accuracy of proprietary radiometric calibration by examining how its performance changes with flight altitude, while also proposing a practical method to correct residual bias after proprietary processing. Thermal imagery is collected with a DJI Matrice 300 RTK equipped with a Zenmuse H20T sensor at 15, 30, 45, and 60 m. Two temperature-controlled plates (TCPs) spanning cold and hot regimes are used to validate three DJI Thermal SDK workflows: (1) default settings; (2) custom settings using measured emissivity, flight altitude, humidity, and reflected apparent temperature (Treflected); and (3) custom settings with per-image tuning of Treflected. Residual bias is subsequently corrected using empirical line calibration (ELC) derived from TCP observations. Across all proprietary workflows, temperature error increases with altitude, indicating progressive gain and offset bias not fully corrected by manufacturer processing. Default settings produce the largest error, with mean absolute error increasing from 4.39°C at 15 m to 11.69°C at 60 m. Custom settings reduce error moderately (3.86°C to 9.43°C), while Treflected tuning improves close-range performance but does not eliminate altitude-dependent degradation (0.99°C to 8.57°C). Application of ELC substantially reduces residual bias across workflows, with best performance achieved by combining Treflected tuning with ELC, resulting in sub-1°C accuracy across altitudes. These results demonstrate that proprietary UAV thermal image processing alone is insufficient to ensure robust temperature retrieval but that a simple empirically calibrated postprocessing workflow using TCPs can mitigate altitude-dependent bias and enable high-accuracy temperature estimation in operational remote sensing applications

Abstract:
Read more... True digital orthophoto maps (TDOMs) serve as foundational geospatial products for applications in land surveying, urban planning, and emergency management. Conventional TDOM generation relies on differential correction, often resulting in cartographic artifacts such as geometric discontinuities, radiometric inconsistencies, and linear feature misalignments. Although recent methods leverage 3D Gaussian splatting to bypass differential correction, their computational demands hinder real-world deployment. Abstract:
Read more... Aqueous minerals, specifically referring to minerals formed through interactions with liquid water, record the history of Mars’s transformation under the influence of internal and external forces. Their types, abundances, and spatial distribution provide clues for studying the ancient environmental conditions and water–rock interactions on Mars. To overcome the limitations of traditional binary classification approaches and better capture the transitional nature of geological processes, this study treats mineral detection as a spatial regression task aimed at predicting the continuous spatial probability distribution of aqueous minerals. To achieve this, we propose a novel geographical environment–driven (GED) prediction method for detecting aqueous minerals. Unlike traditional geostatistical methods, the proposed method uses the spatial locations of known mineral occurrences and integrates multisource geoenvironmental variables from their surrounding neighborhoods to construct sets of variables based on second-dimension association. By applying Lasso regularization, the method identifies and selects key geoenvironmental variables that exhibit strong synergistic effects, thereby establishing direct mapping from multidimensional geoenvironmental variables to the spatial probability distribution of aqueous minerals that reduces the reliance on dense sampling and outperforms traditional interpolation methods, especially in sparsely sampled areas. Comparative experiments against the traditional geostatistical interpolation method demonstrate that the GED method achieves significantly higher predictive accuracy in multiple Martian hydrological landform regions. Uncertainty analysis further indicates that the GED method produces more concentrated prediction errors, achieving a 31.8% reduction in average mean squared error compared to the first dimension association method from 0.2519 to 0.1718. Moreover, the results of the bivariate Moran’s I analysis demonstrate that the proposed GED method, incorporating geographical environmental variables, exhibits higher bivariate Moran’s I values with respect to aqueous mineral distribution than spatial location–only variables. This finding confirms the GED method’s superior capability in characterizing the synergistic correlations between geoenvironmental variables and aqueous minerals under complex and heterogeneous conditions.

Abstract:
Read more... The harmonized Landsat and Sentinel-2 (HLS) version 2.0 product combines the observations of the land surface from the operational land imager (OLI) and multi-spectral instrument (MSI) sensors into a single data set and provides products that include L30, a 30-m OLI nadir bidirectional reflectance distribution function–adjusted reflectance (NBAR), and S30, a 30-m MSI NBAR. Prior research has shown good consistency between L30 and S30 products, but version 2.0 product performance has not been evaluated with a ground network of observations. The Radiometric Calibration Network (RadCalNet) portal provides surface reflectance along with various atmospheric parameters from four instrumented land sites: the Railroad Valley Playa in Nevada; La Crau in France; Baotou in China; and Gobabeb in Namibia. This study presents a rigorous, independent validation of the HLS version 2.0 L30 and S30 products using the RadCalNet. By leveraging RadCalNet’s automated, SI-traceable, hyperspectral bottom-of-atmosphere measurements across four globally distributed sites, we evaluated both the absolute surface reflectance accuracy of the S30 and L30 products and their cross-sensor consistency. Validation results demonstrate strong overall agreement with ground-truth measurements. Excluding localized anomalies at the Baotou site, both L30 and S30 surface reflectances agree with RadCalNet to within 10% at the shortest wavelengths and to within 5% for the near infrared and shortwave infrared bands. Furthermore, by using RadCalNet as a common transfer standard, the analysis reveals an interproduct agreement between L30 and S30 of within 4% across all spectral bands, with the exception of the blue band. A comprehensive summary of the observed differences at each wavelength across all the RadCalNet sites is presented, along with uncertainty estimates. Ultimately, this validation provides quantified, absolute uncertainty metrics that prove the successful radiometric harmonization of the Landsat and Sentinel-2 data streams, equipping the science community with the confidence required to use HLS time series for robust analysis while identifying some future improvements.

Hierarchical Integration of Multi-Source Lidar for Forest Volume Estimation across Individual Trees and Plot Scales

Avaliable online: July 2, 2026
Authors: Gu, Denuo; Pang, Yong; Du, Liming; Kong, Dan;
Abstract:
Read more... Accurate stem volume estimation remains contingent on an analytical tree (i.e., tree felled and sectionally measured to obtain reference stem volume) as the calibration benchmark for remote sensing and allometric equation, thereby limiting scalability and operational efficiency in large-scale forest carbon assessment. To overcome this bottleneck, we propose a hierarchical integration framework for multi-source lidar-based stem volume estimation, termed “hierarchical integration of volume estimation” (HIVE), which establishes a lidar-driven estimation chain linking individual trees to plot-level assessments. Specifically, we first establish a volumetric benchmark from terrestrial laser scanning (TLS) by reconstructing stems with the quantitative structure model (QSM), which serves as a surrogate for analytical trees. We then transfer this benchmark to airborne laser scanning (ALS) by mapping the lidar biomass index (LBI) and tree height to TLS-derived reference volume, effectively coupling nondestructive TLS-based volumetric calibration with broad spatial coverage. Cross-scale calibration and validation then enforce tree-plot consistency, enabling accurate upscaling without reliance on traditional field calibration based on the manual diameter at breast height and tree height measurements. For 41 Japanese larch (Larix kaempferi) trees, TLS-based TreeQSM yielded stem volume estimates consistent with analytical-tree measurements (R2 = 0.97, relative root mean square error [rRMSE] = 12.90%) and outperformed traditional allometric equations (R2 = 0.96, rRMSE = 14.34%). Furthermore, using k-fold cross-validation on 81 TLS-ALS paired trees, the tree-level LBI model showed consistent predictive performance (R2 = 0.81, rRMSE = 27.62%). When transferred to the plot level, stem volume estimates across 67 plots agreed well with field survey references (R2 = 0.72, rRMSE = 23.53%). This approach provides an innovative solution for efficient large-scale forest volume estimation by substantially reducing reliance on traditional field surveys for model calibration and opens new possibilities for carbon stock assessment and sustainable forest management.

Abstract:
Read more... Meeting strict latency budgets for high-performance approximate nearest neighbor (ANN) search remains a critical challenge in remote sensing edge devices, including microsatellites and UAVs, because of stringent limitations in both primary (RAM) and secondary (disk) storage. To address this challenge, we propose Edge-ANN, an efficient ANN framework specifically engineered for storage-efficient edge retrieval. Instead of explicitly storing high-dimensional partitioning hyperplanes, Edge-ANN uses pairs of in-data-set points, termed anchors, together with a scalar offset to define partitions implicitly. Furthermore, within constrained memory budgets, Edge-ANN delivers superior retrieval performance to other mainstream ANN methods under the adopted near-real-time latency budget. Collectively, these results establish Edge-ANN as a practical solution for enabling large-scale, high-performance remote sensing feature retrieval on edge devices with exceptionally constrained storage.
